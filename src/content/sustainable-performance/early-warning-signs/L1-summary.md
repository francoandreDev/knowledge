---
title: "L1 — How do I recognize burnout before it takes me out completely?"
---

- **Tired** and **burned out** are different states, not points on the same simple scale. Tired is resolved by rest — a weekend, a good night's sleep, and the capacity comes back. Burnout doesn't resolve with rest alone, because its core feature isn't depleted energy, it's a change in relationship to the work itself: cynicism, detachment, and a sense that effort no longer produces meaningful results, regardless of how rested you are.
- The three components most burnout research converges on: **exhaustion** (the depleted-energy part, the only one that overlaps with ordinary tiredness), **cynicism/depersonalization** (a growing detachment from or negative attitude toward the work, colleagues, or its purpose), and **reduced efficacy** (a genuine, not just perceived, decline in the sense that your effort accomplishes anything) — burnout specifically requires the latter two, not just the first.
- **This connects directly to `sustainable-performance/myth-linear-output`'s negative-returns region** — burnout is a plausible mechanism behind why hours worked can eventually produce _less_ net output: exhaustion degrades focus, cynicism degrades the care put into work, and reduced efficacy compounds both by removing the motivation to course-correct.
- **Early signs are behavioral and specific, not just a vague feeling of being tired**: increasing cynicism in how you talk about work (to yourself or others), a growing gap between effort and any felt sense of accomplishment, withdrawing from colleagues or collaboration you used to engage with, and needing increasing effort to do things that used to be routine.
- **The trap that delays recognition**: burnout's cynicism component actively works against noticing it — a mindset that's already detached and skeptical of "is this a real problem or am I just being dramatic" tends to dismiss its own early warning signs as not real or not serious enough, which is exactly what lets it progress further before it's addressed.
- This unit is specifically about **recognition**, not treatment or systemic fixes (organizational workload, unsustainable staffing) — those are real and often the actual root cause, but recognizing the pattern early, in yourself or someone you work with, is the prerequisite step before any fix (personal or organizational) can be applied.
- The practical skill: knowing the specific, checkable difference between "I'm tired, I need a weekend" and "I'm burned out, rest alone won't fix this" — and having concrete, named signs to check against, rather than relying on catching it only once it's severe enough to be undeniable.
