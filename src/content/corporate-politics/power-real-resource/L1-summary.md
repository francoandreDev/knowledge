---
title: "L1 — Why doesn't technical merit alone decide who wins in an organization?"
---

- Decisions in an organization aren't made by "whoever is technically correct" — they're made by whoever can get a decision actually adopted, funded, staffed, and sustained. That capability is **power**, and it's a real resource, not a character flaw in the people who have it.
- Power is not the same thing as being liked, being senior, or being loud. It's the ability to make things happen — get resources allocated, get a proposal prioritized, get a team to actually change direction.
- Four common sources of power in an engineering org, none of which require a title:
  - **Formal authority** — you control budget, headcount, or roadmap directly
  - **Expertise** — you're the person whose technical judgment gets deferred to because it's been right before
  - **Relationships** — people trust you and will back your proposal because they trust _you_, independent of the proposal's merits alone
  - **Information** — you know things (what leadership actually cares about, what failed before and why) that let you navigate more effectively than someone without that context
- Being technically right and having zero of these is a genuinely common, genuinely frustrating position — and the frustration is real, but "I was right and it didn't matter" is a description of a power gap, not proof the system is broken or that political skill is beneath serious engineers.
- **Politics, in the neutral sense this unit uses the word, is just the process of getting a group of people with different incentives to align on a decision.** It's not inherently manipulative — treating it as inherently dirty is itself a stance that cedes influence to people who don't share that squeamishness.
- The goal of this unit isn't to teach manipulation — it's to make power visible as a factor so it can be reasoned about deliberately, the same way you'd reason about any other constraint on a system, instead of being repeatedly surprised that being right wasn't sufficient.
