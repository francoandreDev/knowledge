**Two people are trying to sell the exact same water filter pitcher.
The first says: "It has an activated carbon filter, holds 10 cups, and
the plastic is BPA-free." The second says: "Your tap water will taste
like bottled water, and you'll spend about $1,028 less this year than
buying bottled water." Both are describing the same object. Why does
only the second pitch make someone reach for their wallet?**

The first pitch describes what the product _is_. The second describes
what the product _does for the listener_ — a concrete, felt
difference in their own life, expressed in terms they didn't need any
special knowledge to understand.

## The shape of the problem

- A **feature** is a fact about the product: what it contains, how it
  works, what its specifications are. A feature is true regardless of
  who's listening.
- **Value** is what a feature _means_ for a specific listener: the
  outcome they get, expressed in terms they already care about — time
  saved, money saved, a problem that goes away, a feeling that
  changes.
- Feature framing assumes the listener will do the translation work
  themselves — figure out, unprompted, why "activated carbon" matters
  to their life. Most listeners won't do that work, especially if
  they don't already have the background to make the connection. Value
  framing does the translation _for_ them.

## Key terms

- **Feature** — a factual property of a product: what it has, how it
  works, its specifications.
- **Value** — the outcome a feature produces for a specific listener,
  expressed in terms that matter to them without requiring extra
  background knowledge.
- **Translation gap** — the mental work a listener has to do to get
  from a feature to why it matters to them. A wide translation gap
  means most listeners give up before crossing it.
- **Audience-relevant terms** — units of meaning a listener already
  understands and cares about: money, time, comfort, risk, effort —
  as opposed to units specific to the product's own internal
  vocabulary (filter types, technical specs, jargon).

## What this unit covers

L2 works through why feature framing forces the listener to do
translation work most people won't do, and how to find the value
underneath a feature by repeatedly asking "so what does that mean for
them?" L3 works the full water-filter pitch two ways — feature-first
and value-first — with the actual cost comparison worked out in
detail, showing exactly where each framing gains or loses the
listener.
