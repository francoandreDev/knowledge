---
title: "L1 — How do I explain a technical delay without sounding like an excuse?"
---

- A status update has two jobs at once: convey accurate information, and manage the reader's _trust_ in future updates from you. Getting the facts right but delivering them badly can still cost trust — and the reverse (a well-delivered update with fudged facts) costs it even faster once discovered.
- **The instinct that backfires**: when something is late or broken, the natural impulse is to explain _why_ first, then state the impact — which reads as justifying before informing, and makes even a completely legitimate reason sound like an excuse because of the order alone.
- **BLUF applied to bad news**: state the actual status and impact first (what's late, by how much, what it affects), then the reason — the reason is context for someone who wants it, not the thing standing between the reader and the information they actually need.
- A trust-building update contains three things a trust-eroding one usually lacks: a **specific** new estimate (not "soon"), a stated **reason work is now more predictable** (what changed since the last estimate that makes this one more reliable), and an explicit statement of what, if anything, is needed from the reader.
- **Frequency matters as much as content.** A single big surprise ("it's late") erodes trust more than the same delay surfaced incrementally, earlier, before it became a surprise — predictability is itself a component of trust, independent of whether the news is good.
- The failure mode this unit is really about isn't lying — it's **optimism bias reported as fact**: repeatedly saying "should be done Friday" without flagging real uncertainty, and letting Friday quietly become the next Friday. Each individual update might have felt honest in the moment; the pattern reads as unreliable in hindsight.
- The practical discipline: separate what you _know_ from what you're _estimating_, say which is which, and update the estimate the moment new information changes it — not just at the next scheduled check-in.
