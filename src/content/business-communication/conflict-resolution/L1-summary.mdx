Two teams both believe they own the notification preferences
service. Platform has maintained it for two years and considers
changes to it their call. Growth has been shipping onboarding
experiments through it for the last quarter and considers it
essential to their roadmap. Neither team coordinated with the other
before this week's changes, and the two changes collided in
production, causing an incident. Both team leads are frustrated, and
both are convinced the other team is the one overstepping. **Neither
team is lying about their stake in this service. So what's actually
being fought over — is it really "who owns this"?**

</Scenario>

## The shape of the problem

- A **position** is what someone says they want ("we own this
  service"); an **interest** is the actual underlying need driving
  that position (reliability guarantees, or the ability to ship
  experiments on a predictable schedule). Two teams can have
  incompatible positions while having interests that don't actually
  conflict at all.
- Fighting over ownership as a binary, zero-sum label ("it's either
  our service or theirs") makes a resolvable disagreement look
  unresolvable, because both teams are defending a position instead
  of naming what they actually need.
- An **escalation path** is a predefined way to get an unresolved
  conflict in front of someone with the authority to decide — a
  specific person, forum, and criteria for when to invoke it. Without
  one, conflicts either get avoided indefinitely or escalate
  informally and personally, which damages the relationship between
  teams that still have to work together afterward.

## Key terms

- **Position** — the specific thing a person or team says they want
  or are entitled to ("we own this service").
- **Interest** — the underlying need or goal driving a position (why
  owning it actually matters to them).
- **Ownership ambiguity** — a situation where more than one team has
  a legitimate claim to responsibility for the same system, without a
  clear, documented decision about who actually holds it.
- **Escalation path** — a predefined route for getting an unresolved
  disagreement in front of someone with the authority to make a
  binding call, used when direct negotiation between the parties
  genuinely can't resolve it.

## What this unit covers

L2 works through why naming interests instead of defending positions
is what actually unlocks a resolution, and what makes an escalation
path effective instead of just an excuse to avoid a hard
conversation. L3 walks the Scenario's two teams through an
interest-based conversation that finds a real resolution, and a
follow-up disagreement that genuinely needs escalation — showing what
that looks like done well.
