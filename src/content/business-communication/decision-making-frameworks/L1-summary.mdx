Eight people join a meeting to pick a logging vendor. Everyone has an
opinion, the discussion is thoughtful, and 45 minutes later the
meeting ends with "let's take this offline and figure it out." Two
weeks later, a second meeting covers the exact same ground — same
options, same trade-offs, same opinions — because nothing was actually
decided the first time. **Nobody in that room was wrong or
unprepared. So what was actually missing that would have let the same
discussion end in a decision instead of a rerun?**

## The shape of the problem

- A room full of people with opinions isn't the same as a room with
  someone whose job is to _decide_. Without that distinction being
  explicit, a meeting defaults to needing something like consensus —
  and full consensus among 8 people with real trade-offs to weigh is
  genuinely hard to reach in 45 minutes.
- "We'll figure it out" isn't a decision — it's a deferral. And a
  deferral with no owner doesn't get picked back up on its own; it
  waits for someone to schedule another meeting, at which point the
  same people re-litigate the same points.
- A **decision-making framework** exists to answer one specific
  question before the meeting even starts: who is actually going to
  make this call, and who just needs to weigh in versus be told after
  the fact?

Meetings feel like they decide nothing when nobody knows who has both
permission and responsibility to decide.

## Key terms

DACI is for deciding one thing. RACI is for dividing responsibility
across ongoing work.

- **DACI** — a role framework for a single decision: **D**river (runs
  the process, gathers input), **A**pprover (the one person who
  actually decides), **C**ontributors (give input, don't decide),
  **I**nformed (told the outcome, not consulted beforehand).
- **RACI** — a role framework for ongoing work or a project, not a
  single decision: **R**esponsible (does the work), **A**ccountable
  (answers for the outcome), **C**onsulted (gives input), **I**nformed
  (kept in the loop).
- **Decision rights** — an explicit statement of who has the authority
  to make a specific call, separate from who has an opinion about it.
- **Diffusion of responsibility** — the effect where, if a decision
  belongs to "the group," it can end up belonging to no one in
  particular — everyone assumes someone else will make the call.

## What this unit covers

L2 works through DACI and RACI role by role, when each framework
actually fits, and a flowchart of how a decision moves from input to an
actual outcome under DACI. L3 reruns the exact 8-person vendor meeting
with DACI roles explicitly assigned, comparing the time actually spent
against the no-roles version, and works through what each role does
and doesn't have the authority to do.
