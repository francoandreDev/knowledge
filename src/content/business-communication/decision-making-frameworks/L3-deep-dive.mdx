**Before assigning roles: the first version of this meeting cost 8
people 45 minutes each, twice, for zero decision — 720 person-minutes,
12 person-hours, spent. If a DACI structure only needs a focused group
of 4 in the room and lets the rest read a 2-minute summary afterward,
roughly how much of that 720 person-minutes would actually survive?**

## Assigning the roles for the vendor decision

| Role         | Who                                                                        | Why                                                                        |
| ------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Driver       | The engineer who proposed evaluating a new logging vendor                  | Already did the initial research — natural fit to run the process          |
| Approver     | The engineering director                                                   | Owns the budget line and the tooling decision for the org                  |
| Contributors | 2 senior engineers with hands-on experience with the top candidate vendors | Their input on real trade-offs is what the decision actually needs         |
| Informed     | The remaining 4 people from the original meeting                           | Affected by the outcome, but their input isn't what the decision hinges on |

Nothing here says the other 4 people's opinions don't matter — it says
their input isn't the _deciding_ factor, and being told the outcome
(with the reasoning) serves them just as well as sitting through a
live debate would have.

<Checkpoint question="One of the 4 Informed people from the original meeting complains that they were left out of the decision entirely. Based on what the Informed role actually is, is that complaint accurate?">

Not quite — being Informed isn't the same as being left out. The role
exists specifically so people who don't need to shape the decision
still get the outcome and the reasoning behind it, rather than finding
out secondhand or not at all. What they didn't get was a seat in the
live debate, because their input wasn't what the decision hinged on —
but a short written summary is still owed to them, and skipping it
would be a real failure, not a feature of the role.

</Checkpoint>

## What actually happens under this structure

1. The Driver spends time gathering input from the 2 Contributors
   individually, before scheduling anything — not in a group meeting.
2. The Driver frames 2–3 concrete options with trade-offs, based on
   that input, and brings a recommendation.
3. A single focused meeting — Driver, Approver, and the 2
   Contributors, 4 people total — reviews the framing and the
   recommendation.
4. The Approver decides, on the spot, because the hard part (surfacing
   the trade-offs) already happened before this meeting.
5. The Driver sends a short written summary — the decision and the
   reasoning — to the 4 Informed people. No meeting required for them.

## Counting the time, before and after

A person-minute means one person spending one minute. Eight people in
a 45-minute meeting cost `8 x 45 = 360` person-minutes. Doing it twice
costs 720. The unit matters because a meeting's cost grows with people
and time, not only clock time.

| Version             | Who's involved                                                   | Time per person | Total (person-minutes) |
| ------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------- | ---------------------- |
| No roles (original) | All 8, twice (45-min meetings)                                   | 90 min each     | **720**                |
| With DACI           | 4 in the decision meeting (20 min) + 4 reading a summary (2 min) | 20 min / 2 min  | **88**                 |

Verified: `4 people × 20 minutes + 4 people × 2 minutes = 80 + 8 = 88`
person-minutes — an **88% reduction** from the original 720, and
critically, this version actually ends with a decision instead of a
second meeting on the calendar.

The percentage reduction is `(720 - 88) / 720 = 632 / 720`, about
`0.88`, or 88%. In plain words: most of the original cost was people
waiting in a meeting where they did not need to decide.

<Checkpoint question="The 720 person-minutes in the original meeting and the 88 person-minutes under DACI both involve the same 8 people and the same decision. What actually changed to produce most of that savings — did people get faster at talking, or something else?">

Something else — nobody got faster at talking, and the decision meeting
itself wasn't even shortened by much per person. What changed is who
had to be in the room and for how long: instead of all 8 sitting
through two full 45-minute meetings, only 4 people (Driver, Approver,
2 Contributors) needed a real meeting, and even that one was shorter
because the hard part — gathering input and framing trade-offs — had
already happened beforehand, individually, rather than live in front
of everyone. The other 4 spent 2 minutes reading a summary instead of
90 minutes sitting through two meetings. The savings come from
reducing how many people's time a single decision consumes, not from
making the conversation itself faster.

</Checkpoint>

## What if the decision genuinely needs more than 2 Contributors?

**This example fixed the Contributors count at 2 — what happens to the
total if a decision genuinely needs input from, say, 4 people with
specialized knowledge, not 2?** The 88-minute total above isn't a fixed
constant; it scales with how many people are actually in the decision
meeting. Try recomputing the total for a 4-person Contributors group
(so 6 people in the decision meeting instead of 4) — does the
percentage saved compared to the no-roles version change much, or does
it stay close to the same order of magnitude?

## Failure modes

| Failure mode                                                              | What it gets wrong                                                                                                                             |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Naming a Driver but never naming an Approver                              | The meeting still defaults to needing consensus — a Driver without an Approver just organizes the same deadlock more neatly                    |
| Making "the whole team" the Approver                                      | This isn't DACI — a group Approver reintroduces exactly the diffusion of responsibility DACI exists to remove                                  |
| Treating Contributors as if they have veto power                          | Their input shapes the decision; if their disagreement can block it outright, they're functioning as co-Approvers, and that should be explicit |
| Skipping the Informed step because "they'll hear about it eventually"     | Silent decisions erode trust — a short summary costs the Driver a few minutes and prevents people finding out secondhand                       |
| Using DACI for something that's really an ongoing responsibility question | A single-decision framework applied to "who owns this recurring task" produces an awkward fit — that's what RACI is actually for               |

Concrete examples make the failure modes easier to spot: veto power
means one Contributor can block the decision; co-Approvers means two
people both think they must approve; secondhand means people hear the
decision through rumor or fragments instead of a clear summary.
