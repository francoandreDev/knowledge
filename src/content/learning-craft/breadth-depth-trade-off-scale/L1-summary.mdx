## Is breadth just what you settle for when time runs out?

**If nobody can go deep on everything, is staying shallow a failure?** No. For
most calls breadth is the right default, and depth is the exception a specific
decision has to earn.

- **Breadth:** working familiarity with a domain. You know it exists, roughly
  what it does, who owns it, and enough vocabulary to ask a sharp question.
- **Depth:** the ability to reason through the domain yourself and verify a
  claim without relying on anyone's judgment but your own.
- **Borrowed judgment:** acting on an expert's reasoning instead of
  re-deriving it, while still checking that what they said holds together.
- **Domain expert:** whoever has actually built depth in the specific
  mechanism at play, whatever their title.

| Idea               | In the clinic                                  | At work                                            |
| ------------------ | ---------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------- |
| Breadth            | Knows the warning signs and who treats what    | Knows a system exists, who owns it, its vocabulary |
| Depth              | Can diagnose and treat the condition herself   | Can verify a claim about it without help           |
| Borrowed judgment  | Acts on the specialist's advice after checking | Acts on the owner's explanation after checking     |
| Bluffing           | Nods along and guesses at a diagnosis          | Guesses at a term because it sounds plausible      |
| Over-investigating | Orders every test for a cough                  | Spends days on a call a short chat would settle    |

The two failure modes look opposite but share a cause: treating breadth versus
depth as something that happens to you, instead of a decision.

## What does the same fork look like in a job?

**What if the decision is a release tomorrow morning and the domain is one you
have never touched?** Priya is an engineer on a platform team. At 5pm a VP asks
her to confirm that a mobile team's background-sync change will not drain
battery for millions of users. She has never worked on mobile power management.

Her options are not "fake it" or "refuse." There is a third: decide quickly
whether this call needs her own depth, and if it does not, borrow judgment from
the one person who has it. Whether the call needs depth depends on three things
(stakes, reversibility, time-to-signal), which L2 builds.

## Key terms

- **Stakes:** how much damage a wrong call does.
- **Reversibility:** how cheaply a wrong call can be undone once found.
- **Time-to-signal:** how fast a wrong call would show itself before it does
  real damage.
- **Sharp question:** a question specific enough that the answer can be
  checked, such as "does this hold the radio awake long enough to matter?"
  instead of "is this safe?"

This is the synthesis unit of the track. It uses the judgment skills from
earlier units, weighing credibility and staying calibrated, on a live colleague
instead of a document.
