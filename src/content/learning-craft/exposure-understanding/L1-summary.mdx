## What is the difference between meeting something and being able to use it?

**Watching ten cooking shows does not mean you can cook the dish. Why not?**
Watching is contact with the material. Cooking is using it.

- **Exposure** is encountering information: reading it, hearing it, watching it.
- **Understanding** is being able to use it: produce it with the source closed,
  explain it in your own words, or apply it to a case you have not seen.
- Both leave a feeling of "I know this", which is why they get confused.

| Signal               | What it sounds like            | What it actually proves           |
| -------------------- | ------------------------------ | --------------------------------- |
| "It sounds familiar" | "I have seen this before"      | Exposure happened                 |
| "I recognize it"     | "Yes, that answer looks right" | Recognition, with the page's help |
| "I can do it cold"   | "7×8 is 56, no sheet needed"   | The skill can be used             |

## Why does the fifth read feel better than the first?

**If nothing new was learned, where did Priya's growing confidence come from?**
From the page getting easier to process, not from the skill getting stronger.

- The **fluency illusion** is mistaking "this is easy to read" for "I can
  produce this". Every extra pass makes reading smoother.
- Passive exposure (rereading, highlighting, watching a tutorial) is low effort
  and produces this feeling reliably.
- Active recall (closing the source and reproducing it) is harder, and gives an
  honest answer: it works or it visibly does not.

| &nbsp;                    | Passive exposure (rereading) | Active recall (tested cold)        |
| ------------------------- | ---------------------------- | ---------------------------------- |
| Effort while doing it     | Low, feels smooth            | High, feels slow                   |
| What it measures          | How easy the page is to read | Whether it can be produced unaided |
| Priya, Monday / Wednesday | 90% felt ready               | 40% actually correct               |

None of this is an argument against reading. Priya had to read the table before
she could be tested on it. The mistake is stopping at the read and treating a
smooth one as a passed test. After any exposure, close the source and try to
reproduce it before moving on. The fuller method for that is the
[Feynman technique](/learning-craft/feynman-technique/).
