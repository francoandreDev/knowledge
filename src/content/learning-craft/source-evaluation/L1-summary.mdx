## What is the difference between the place a claim started and the places that repeat it?

**The teacher's posted schedule and a classmate's screenshot of it both "say" Monday. Are they the same kind of source?** No. One is the original, the other is a copy of it.

| Term             | Plain version                                             | School-life example                                        |
| ---------------- | --------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------- |
| Primary source   | The original place that can settle the claim              | The teacher's posted schedule                              |
| Secondary source | Someone reporting, summarizing or repeating that original | A classmate forwarding a screenshot                        |
| Verification     | Comparing the claim with something you can check          | Opening the course page instead of trusting the group chat |
| Corroboration    | Real, independent support, not the same message copied    | Two teachers saying it separately beats two forwards       |

- Neither kind is better in general. A summary is often faster and good enough. They fail in different ways: a primary source can be hard to read, a secondary one can be wrong or out of date.
- Each layer between you and the original is a chance for a detail to be dropped or changed, and later layers tend to copy the earlier ones instead of going back to the original.

## What should you ask before you trust a specific claim?

**You cannot trace everything you read. So which claims deserve the effort?** Ask three questions, and let the answers decide how much more checking is worth doing.

| #   | Question                                                      | What it decides                                    |
| --- | ------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------- |
| 1   | Is this a primary or a secondary source?                      | How much interpretation has already happened to it |
| 2   | Can I trace it back to something I could check myself?        | Whether checking is even possible right now        |
| 3   | How fast does this kind of claim go stale, and how old is it? | Whether age is a red flag here                     |

Rule of thumb: if being wrong costs little and is easy to undo, a quick look is enough. If being wrong changes an important decision, costs money, affects health or is hard to undo, spend more effort tracing the claim.
